# Mahabharata kings: Mykytiuk-style identification register (draft v1)

Method: Mykytiuk (2004), *Identifying Biblical Persons in Northwest Semitic Inscriptions of 1200–539 B.C.E.*,
adapted from inscriptions to Vedic texts that are older than, and independent of, the epic.
Mykytiuk sorts identifications into six categories, from unmistakable to disqualified. The labels below
are an adaptation; check his exact category definitions (book, and *Maarav* 16.1, 2009) before citing.

Three checks per person:
1. **Source:** is the independent text older than the epic and reliably transmitted?
2. **Setting:** right period (Late Vedic, c. 1200–500 BCE) and right society (Kuru / Pañcāla)?
3. **Identifying marks:** name, father (patronymic), title, relatives, associates, events. More matching marks = stronger.

Grades: **Strong** (3+ marks, setting fits) · **Reasonable** (2 marks, or 3 with a conflict) ·
**Possible** (name plus 1 weak mark) · **Name only** · **None** (no independent mention) · **Disqualified** (same name, clearly a different person).

## The pattern

- **Strong or reasonable:** the generations **before** the war (Pratīpa's sons Devāpi, Śaṃtanu, Bālhika; Dhṛtarāṣṭra)
  and **after** it (Parikṣit, Janamejaya and his brothers), plus a Pañcāla link (Śikhaṇḍin Yājñasena).
- **None:** the **war generation itself**: the Pāṇḍavas, Duryodhana, Karṇa, Droṇa.
- So the dynasty that frames the epic is independently attested. The heroes at its centre are not.
  That is the main finding to report, and the same pattern as Troy (real city, unattested heroes).
