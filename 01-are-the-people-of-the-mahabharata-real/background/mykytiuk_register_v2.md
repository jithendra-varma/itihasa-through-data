# Mahabharata kings: identification register (v2, references checked)

v2 replaces v1. Every Vedic reference below was checked against Macdonell & Keith, *Vedic Index of Names
and Subjects* (1912), full text on archive.org (`vedicindexofname01macduoft`, `vedicindexofname02macduoft`).
"VI" = Vedic Index. Epic cross-references still need checking against the Critical Edition.

## Mykytiuk's rules (confirmed)

- Three criteria, all required: (1) **authentic data**, (2) **matching setting** (time and place),
  (3) enough **identifying marks** (name, father's name, title, place, family line) that the two cannot be different people.
- **Three matching marks** with the setting fitting = "virtually certain to reliable" (**grade 3**).
  **Two marks** = "reasonable but uncertain" (**grade 2**). **One mark (name only) is not enough.**
- Six categories in all, "from unmistakable to disqualified". Forgeries are disqualified.
  Exact names of the lowest categories still to confirm from the 2004 book.
- **Important:** Mykytiuk uses only **inscriptions**. He excludes figures known only from texts copied over
  centuries. **Applied strictly, no Mahabharata king can be identified at all**, because India has no inscriptions
  before Ashoka (3rd century BCE). Everything below is an **adaptation**: Vedic texts are treated as the
  independent source, because they are older than the epic and were transmitted orally with high fidelity.

Grades used here: **3**, **2** (Mykytiuk) · **Weak** (two marks but a mark conflicts) ·
**Name only** · **Disqualified** (same name, a different person) · **None** (no Vedic mention). The last four are my labels.

## What changed from v1

- **Only Janamejaya reaches grade 3.** Parikṣit, Bālhika and Dhṛtarāṣṭra drop to grade 2.
- **Śaṃtanu, Devāpi, Śikhaṇḍin and Kṛṣṇa drop to Weak**, because the Vedic person's father or role contradicts the epic.
- **Pratīpa drops to Name only. Śatānīka is disqualified.**
- All four "check this" items from v1 are now resolved (Pratīpa, Vyāsa, Śatānīka, Śikhaṇḍin).

## The finding, restated

- **Securely attested:** one king, Janamejaya, the generation **after** the war, with his brothers.
- **Reasonably attested:** his father Parikṣit, and two earlier Kuru names (Bālhika, Dhṛtarāṣṭra), each with a doubt.
- **The war generation** (the Pāṇḍavas, Duryodhana, Karṇa, Droṇa) has **no** independent attestation.
- The epic's frame (its narration to Janamejaya) is anchored to a real late Vedic court.
  Its earlier generations look like **real Vedic names assembled into one family tree later**,
  which is what the Vedic Index argues for Devāpi, Śaṃtanu and Bālhika.
