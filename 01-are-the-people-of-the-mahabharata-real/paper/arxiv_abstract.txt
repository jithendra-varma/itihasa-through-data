Whether the people of the Mahabharata existed is usually argued case by case. We replace the argument with a test. Every person in the epic's main lineages and embedded stories (n = 107) was compared with every person record in the older Vedic corpus as catalogued in the Vedic Index of Names and Subjects (n = 2,051). The method combines oral-tradition criticism (Vansina, Henige), Mykytiuk's identifying-mark criteria for ancient persons, Fellegi-Sunter probabilistic record linkage with frequency weights, and a Bayesian posterior mapped onto Mykytiuk's grades. A protocol fixing assumptions, rules and five validation tests was committed before any result was seen, and every later change is recorded as an amendment. The first version failed its false-positive test (1.3% against a 1% limit). Version 3, which uses a clean digital transcription of the Index, requires the Vedic text to state the same kind of relationship as the epic, and disqualifies tribes and conflicting roles, passes all pre-set tests: 21 matches against at most 11 in 1,000 permutations (p = 0.001), 0.48% false positives over 219,343 wrong-name pairs, and 7 of 9 known identities recovered. Against hand review it reaches 0.76 precision and 0.84 recall (0.95 precision on identity alone). A blind second review agreed on every identity decision (Cohen's kappa = 1.00; 0.77 when legendary figures are separated), and every relationship used was checked against the Critical Edition. The results are asymmetric. The generation after the war is securely attested: Janamejaya Pariksita, his brothers and his priest Indrota appear in the Satapatha Brahmana with the same expiation episode the epic tells. Earlier Kuru figures are real Vedic names re-arranged into a new genealogy, and Rigvedic tribes became brothers. The war generation itself - the Pandavas, the Kauravas, Bhisma, Drona, Karna and Draupadi - has no independent attestation.
